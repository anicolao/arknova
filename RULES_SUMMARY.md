# Ark Nova Rules Summary

This is an implementation-oriented summary of the **Ark Nova base game** for
1–4 players. It is a reference, not a replacement for the official rulebook,
glossary, card text, or current errata.

Primary references:

- [Official English rulebook (Capstone Games)](https://capstone-games.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/Ark-Nova-Rulebook.pdf)
- [Official errata and FAQ (Feuerland Spiele)](https://www.feuerland-spiele.de/fileadmin/game/Arche_Nova/Arche_Nova_FAQ_V2_EN_A4_low.pdf)
- [Publisher's Ark Nova page](https://www.feuerland-spiele.de/en/)

## Objective

Build a successful zoo by combining:

- **Appeal**, primarily earned from animals, which also determines break income.
- **Conservation points**, primarily earned by supporting conservation projects.
- **Reputation**, which controls access to cards in the display and awards bonuses.

Appeal and conservation advance toward one another on opposing tracks. The end
of the game is triggered when one player's counters reach or pass one another.
Final score combines their relative positions, then adds end-game scoring cards
and other final effects. The highest score wins.

## Important Terms

- **Strength:** The numbered slot, 1–5, occupied by an action card. It limits
  what that action can do.
- **Upgraded action:** The stronger side of an action card. It is unlocked by
  reaching upgrade spaces on the conservation track or by map-specific effects.
- **Zoo card:** An animal, sponsor, or conservation-project card.
- **Display:** The six face-up zoo cards beside the main deck.
- **Within reputation range:** A display slot at or below the player's current
  reputation reach.
- **Snap:** Take one specified display card directly into hand.
- **Icon:** A symbol supplied by cards, partner zoos, universities, or other
  effects. Icons remain available while their source remains in play.
- **Break:** A shared upkeep phase triggered when the break token reaches the
  end of its track. It is not the end of a round in the usual sense; a break can
  happen during any player's action.
- **X-token:** A token spent to increase an action's strength by 1. A player may
  hold at most 5 and may spend multiple tokens on one action.

## Setup

### Shared area

1. Place the main board, association board, zoo-card deck, building tiles,
   partner zoos, universities, tokens, and money within reach.
2. Shuffle the zoo cards and deal six face up into the numbered display.
3. Randomly place three base conservation projects on the association board.
4. Place the break token on the starting space appropriate to the player count.
5. Establish a common supply and choose a starting player.

### Each player

1. Take a zoo map and all pieces of one color.
2. Put the appeal, conservation, and reputation counters on their starting
   spaces.
3. Place player tokens on the marked spaces of the zoo map. These uncover
   bonuses and income as they are removed.
4. Put one association worker in the available-worker area. Place the remaining
   workers on their unlock spaces.
5. Arrange the five action cards below the zoo map in their prescribed starting
   order, basic side up. Their slots establish initial strengths 1–5.
6. Draw eight zoo cards and keep four. Discard the other four to the discard
   pile. For a first game, the official starter hands may be used instead.
7. Take the starting money and any map-specific setup benefits.

Resolve setup bonuses and determine turn order as directed by the chosen maps
or digital scenario.

## Turn Structure

Players take turns clockwise. On a normal turn, choose exactly one of these:

1. **Use an action card.** Resolve it at its current strength, then move that
   card to slot 1. Shift every card that was below it one slot to the right.
2. **Take an X-token.** Move any action card to slot 1 without resolving it,
   shift the lower cards, and gain one X-token.

An action card's strength is its slot number plus any X-tokens spent on it.
Bonuses may modify that value. Unless an effect says otherwise, unused strength
is lost.

Effects gained during an action are normally resolved immediately. If several
effects are gained together, the active player chooses their order unless a
card or rule specifies one.

## The Five Actions

### Cards

Acquire zoo cards and manipulate the display.

The available choice depends on action strength and whether the action has been
upgraded. Lower strengths generally draw blindly from the deck; higher strengths
draw more cards, retain more of them, or take a visible card. Drawing multiple
cards and discarding down is one action, not several separate draws.

On the basic side, access to face-up cards is restricted by reputation range.
The upgraded side improves card efficiency, expands display access, and enables
the strongest display options. Whenever a display card is removed, slide cards
toward the lower-numbered slots and refill the empty high slot from the deck.

Cards in hand have no effect and supply no icons until played.

### Build

Pay money to place buildings on empty hexes of the zoo map.

General placement rules:

- A building must fit entirely on legal, empty spaces.
- Except for the first building, it must touch an existing building along at
  least one hex edge.
- A standard enclosure costs 2 money per occupied hex.
- Kiosks and pavilions are size-one buildings and cost 2 money each.
- A building cannot cover rock or water unless its tile or an effect permits it.
- Covering a printed placement bonus resolves that bonus immediately.
- A building may not be moved after placement unless an effect explicitly says
  so.

The basic Build action places one standard enclosure, kiosk, or pavilion whose
size does not exceed the action strength. The upgraded action can place one or
more different buildings with total size no greater than the action strength
and can construct special enclosures or other unlocked special buildings.

New standard enclosures enter play **empty-side up**. When an animal occupies
one, flip it to its occupied side. A normal enclosure holds only one animal.

### Animals

Play animal cards from hand into the zoo.

For every animal played:

1. Meet all printed prerequisites, including continent, animal-class,
   reputation, partner-zoo, and other icon requirements.
2. Have an appropriate empty enclosure. Its size must meet the animal's
   enclosure requirement and it must touch any required number of rock and/or
   water hexes.
3. Pay the animal's cost, applying partner-zoo and other discounts.
4. Place the animal card in the zoo, occupy the enclosure, gain its appeal and
   other printed rewards, then resolve its ability.

The basic action plays at most one animal whose required action strength is no
greater than the action strength. The upgraded action may play one or two
animals whose combined strength requirements do not exceed the action strength.
Each animal is fully resolved before the next is played.

Special enclosures, such as the petting zoo and reptile house, follow their own
capacity rules and may hold multiple eligible animals.

### Association

Assign available association workers to tasks on the association board.

The four standard task strengths are:

| Strength | Task |
| ---: | --- |
| 2 | Gain reputation |
| 3 | Take an available partner zoo |
| 4 | Take an available university |
| 5 | Support a conservation project |

A worker used for a task remains on the association board until the next break.
If a task space is occupied, additional workers may be required as indicated by
the board.

The basic action performs one task. The upgraded action may perform one or more
**different** tasks whose combined strengths do not exceed the action strength,
using the required worker or workers for each task.

To support a conservation project:

1. Meet one unoccupied support level on a base project or a project previously
   played from a player's hand.
2. Place a player token on that level.
3. Gain the indicated conservation points.
4. If supporting a project from hand, first add it to the association board,
   then support one valid level yourself.

The same player cannot occupy more than one level of the same project unless a
specific effect permits it.

### Sponsors

Play sponsor cards or deliberately advance the break.

The basic action plays at most one sponsor whose strength requirement does not
exceed the action strength. The upgraded action may play one or more sponsors
whose combined requirements do not exceed the action strength. All printed
prerequisites and costs must still be met.

Instead of playing a sponsor, a player may use the alternative shown on the
action card: advance the break token by the action's strength and gain the
corresponding money. If this reaches the end of the break track, finish the
current effect and resolve a break immediately.

Sponsor cards stay in the zoo and can provide immediate effects, ongoing
abilities, icons, income, or final scoring. A card's exact timing symbol controls
when its effect applies.

## Reputation and the Card Display

Reputation controls which numbered display slots a player can reach. When an
effect allows taking or playing a card within reputation range, compare the
display slot with the player's current reputation reach.

Crossing marked reputation spaces grants their printed bonuses. Reputation has
a maximum; excess reputation is normally lost unless an effect says otherwise.

When a display card is discarded or taken, close the gap toward slot 1 and deal
a replacement into slot 6. At a break, cards in the designated lowest slots are
discarded before the display is refilled.

## Zoo Map Rules

The zoo map contains:

- buildable hexes;
- rock and water hexes, which are not normally buildable but provide adjacency;
- placement bonuses;
- edge bonuses unlocked by building adjacent to them;
- player-token spaces that unlock workers, action upgrades, association access,
  income, reputation range, and other benefits.

A tile is adjacent to rock or water if at least one of its occupied hexes shares
an edge with the corresponding printed hex. Count distinct required hexes when
an animal specifies multiple rock or water adjacencies.

Kiosks generate break income based on nearby occupied buildings as shown by the
map/rules. Pavilions grant appeal when built.

## Break Track and Break Phase

Card effects and the Sponsors alternative advance the shared break token. When
it reaches the final space, complete the currently resolving effect, then pause
normal play and perform the break.

Resolve the break procedure in rulebook order:

1. Players discard down to their current hand limit. The normal limit is 3;
   upgrading Cards increases it to 5.
2. Discard the designated low-numbered cards from the display, slide the
   remaining cards down, and refill the display.
3. Return all association workers from the association board to their owners.
4. Resolve income. Each player gains appeal-based income plus income from
   kiosks, sponsor cards, uncovered map spaces, and other income effects.
5. Refill or refresh shared supplies as directed, then reset the break token to
   the player-count starting position.

The player whose turn caused the break does not receive another action. Play
continues with the next player after the break.

## Appeal, Conservation, and Bonuses

Whenever appeal, conservation, or reputation advances, resolve crossed or
reached bonuses immediately. If several bonuses are triggered, the active
player chooses their order unless the board specifies otherwise.

Conservation-track bonuses include action-card upgrades, additional workers,
map tokens, reputation, and other rewards. When offered a choice, take one legal
option; a reward that cannot be used is normally lost unless its text permits
otherwise.

Appeal may decrease. Conservation points normally do not decrease unless an
effect explicitly instructs it.

## End of Game

The end is triggered immediately after a player's appeal and conservation
counters have reached or passed one another in their scoring relationship.
Finish the current action and all effects it caused.

Every other player then receives one final turn. The triggering player does not
take another normal turn.

### Final scoring

1. Resolve final-scoring cards and all other end-game effects.
2. Add or subtract their appeal-equivalent value as instructed.
3. Use the main board's scoring relationship to compare the final appeal
   position with the appeal value associated with the final conservation
   position. The difference is the player's final score.

Scores may be negative. Highest score wins. Ties are broken by the combined
value of supported conservation projects according to the official tie-break
rule; if still tied, share the victory.

## Solo Game

The solo game uses the reverse side of the solo tile as a turn countdown and
does not use the normal break-triggered multiplayer pacing. The player attempts
to achieve a positive final score before the available actions expire.

Solo setup, break-marker movement, card-display cycling, and difficulty targets
differ from multiplayer and should be implemented from the dedicated solo rules
on page 20 of the official rulebook rather than inferred from the multiplayer
flow.

## Rules Priorities and Interaction Guidance

Use this order when rules appear to conflict:

1. Explicit card or map text.
2. The official glossary entry for that card, ability, or icon.
3. Current official errata/FAQ.
4. General rulebook rules.
5. This summary.

Additional principles:

- Resolve an effect as fully as possible, but never invent a legal target or
  payment when none exists.
- Costs and prerequisites must be satisfied before an action is performed.
- A discount cannot reduce a cost below zero.
- Icons are not spent unless an effect explicitly says they are.
- Moving a card to action slot 1 happens after resolving the chosen action.
- Gaining an action as an effect does not necessarily move its action card;
  follow the wording of the effect.
- Card-specific limits and timing override general action limits.

### Corrected enclosure-selection rule

For releasing an animal into the wild or moving one into a newly built special
enclosure, official errata requires flipping the **smallest possible occupied
enclosure that meets every enclosure requirement**, including rock and water.
If none does, flip the smallest occupied enclosure that at least meets the size
requirement. If none meets even the size requirement, do not flip an enclosure.

## Suggested Engine Model

For a digital implementation, keep these concepts separate:

- immutable card definitions and localized text;
- player-owned card instances and their zones;
- action-card slot order, upgraded state, and temporary strength modifiers;
- zoo-map occupancy and hex-edge adjacency;
- persistent icons versus spendable resources;
- queued effects with explicit source, target, timing, and optionality;
- break advancement as an interrupt scheduled after the current effect resolves;
- end-game trigger state and remaining final turns;
- rule-version and errata identifiers.

Card abilities should be modeled as discrete effects rather than parsed from
display text. The official glossary remains the source of truth for exceptional
cards and ambiguous interactions.
